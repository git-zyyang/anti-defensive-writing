# Develop the content; judge the actual prose

This guide is the portable expression method used by the skill. Read it with the current task and genre in mind. It does not impose a standard length, paragraph count, or visible review ritual.

## 1. Establish what the passage should accomplish

Identify the judgment the reader should understand, the action they should be able to take, or the experience the scene should create. Decide which facts, citations, comparisons, or details serve that purpose and where they belong.

Use an author-approved passage or an appropriate published model when one is available. Read a complete relevant passage rather than borrowing labels such as “natural” or “rigorous.” Notice where the judgment appears, how reasons unfold, how evidence participates, and how sentences carry the reader forward. Transfer that organization without importing the model's facts or distinctive phrasing.

Previous feedback has a scope. A preference for one paper or one scene does not become a universal ban. Distinguish the author's confirmed text from a candidate produced by an assistant.

## 2. Develop the strongest supported version

During ideation, identify the useful question and the relationships worth explaining. Before recommending abandonment or narrower scope, examine substantive overlap, factual conflict, logical inconsistency, or actual feasibility.

During drafting, perform the passage's positive task. A theoretical paragraph explains a relationship; a results paragraph interprets a finding; a proposal connects a problem to an investigation; a scene makes something happen to a character. A reminder to “investigate further” cannot replace an explanation already supported by the material.

During review, evaluate a criticism by its location, basis, and implications. Improve the argument or evidence where possible. Correct errors and their downstream consequences at the affected points.

## 3. Match the edit to the problem

| Problem | Useful response |
|---|---|
| Factual, logical, reference, or wording error | Repair the relationship and affected dependencies. |
| Repetition or self-defense with no reader function | Remove or combine it; reread for lost information or voice. |
| Accurate but generic prose | Develop the particular relationship, comparison, condition, or consequence supported by the material. |
| Plenty of material with no center | State the passage's judgment and reorganize the evidence around it. |
| An effective, substantial passage | Preserve its force, development, and rhythm; repair only identifiable defects. |

Adding detail requires material. If a source is missing, identify the precise gap in the editing note. An authorized research task may retrieve it. Never manufacture evidence to make a paragraph feel fuller.

## 4. Work from passages to sentences

**Development.** Give the central relationship enough space. A list of concepts or actions does not explain how they connect. Concrete detail and theoretical depth can coexist.

**Continuity.** Each sentence should pick up a question, concept, event, or feeling already available to the reader and develop it. A transition word cannot manufacture a logical relationship.

**Agents and objects.** Make it clear who does what, what is compared, and what pronouns refer to. The writer's background knowledge is not automatically the reader's.

**Additions and deletions.** Before cutting, check for loss of information, inference, evidence, conditions, direction, voice, or rhythm. Before expanding, identify the missing explanation or experience. Do not optimize for a target reduction in words.

**The whole work.** Read the sections that actually exist: abstract, introduction, analysis, conclusion, or linked scenes. A repeated limitation can dominate several sections even when paraphrased positively. Place it according to each section's function, retaining conditions needed by independently read units.

**Dependencies.** If a definition, claim, name, time, or event changes, inspect its affected uses. A repaired problem returning in different words is still a recurrence.

## 5. Four local language checks

These checks concern meaning and function:

- Does a comment such as “this is noteworthy” have an identifiable object?
- Do adjacent sentences repeat a template that obscures different relationships?
- Does a contrast answer a real distinction, objection, or change in understanding?
- Does a generic evaluation obscure concrete information already present?

Parallel comparisons, conceptual contrasts, deliberate repetition, literary omission, and established disciplinary forms may be appropriate. Do not convert these checks into bans on punctuation, long sentences, three-item lists, or particular words. In English, preserve the target language's idiom and disciplinary register rather than applying Chinese stylistic habits.

## 6. Preserve what each genre is trying to achieve

| Genre | The work should deliver |
|---|---|
| Academic argument | A developed relationship or judgment supported by sources and evidence, with appropriate inferential strength. |
| Empirical reporting | The meaning of verified findings, including consequential uncertainty and adverse results. |
| Books and teaching | A continuous development of knowledge, history, and theory that helps the reader understand. |
| Proposals and policy | A credible relationship between a problem, the actors involved, and the proposed investigation or action. |
| Fiction and essays | Desire, choice, relationships, events, voice, and felt experience. |
| Reports, slides, and public communication | A clear account of the object, action, judgment, and significance, distributed sensibly across the medium. |
| Everyday communication | The requested message with appropriate courtesy and responsibility. |

## 7. Check the result without relying on the editing note

First read only the finished passage. Identify its judgment, reasons, or experience, and the first point where a reader must guess, restart, or loses the thread. Then compare with the source and protected material.

Judge whether the concrete comparison, reasoning, evidence, or scene actually advances understanding. Correctness, fluency, and shorter length alone do not prove improvement. Stop when the task is complete and no concrete defect remains. User acceptance, language review, factual verification, and formatting checks remain distinct kinds of evidence.
